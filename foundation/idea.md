# Sentinel-8D: Statistical Root-Cause Traceback & AIAG 8D Corrective Action

*Full problem framing, rationale, scope, risks, deliverables, and how to present this in interviews. This is the "why and what"; `execution.md` is the "how".*

---

## 1. Context & background

Discrete manufacturing moves a part through a **routing** — an ordered sequence of operations (turn, mill, drill, deburr, finish, inspect). Each operation has controllable **process parameters** (feed, speed, pressure, temperature, setpoints) and introduces its own variation. Quality is usually judged at **final inspection**, at the end of the line.

This creates a structural gap: **the station where a defect is *detected* is almost never the station where it was *created*.** A final-inspection reject for an out-of-tolerance bore might originate three operations upstream in a feed-rate drift that nobody flagged, because that upstream step had no in-line gate. Closing this gap — walking a final failure back to its true origin — is the core job of root-cause analysis, and the discipline that formalizes it is the **8D (Eight Disciplines)** corrective-action process.

Most portfolio "RCA projects" stop at a fishbone diagram and a plausible story. This project goes further: it **proves** the root cause statistically on real multi-station data, then documents it in an industry-standard 8D — the exact artifact a supplier ships to an OEM to close a nonconformance.

## 2. Problem statement

> On a multi-step machining line, a fraction of finished parts fail final quality control. The failures are caught at the end of the line, but the responsible **operation** and the responsible **process parameter** are unknown. Until the defect is traced to its origin, corrective action is guesswork: scrap continues, cost accrues, and the same nonconformance recurs on the next lot.

**Primary question (answered with data):**
*Which upstream station, and which parameter, most strongly drives the final-QC failure — and how large and how certain is that effect?*

**Supporting sub-questions:**
1. What is the dominant failure mode, and what is its baseline rate and volume? (defines the "how big is this" in the 8D problem description)
2. Which parameters differ significantly between passing and failing parts? (screening)
3. After controlling for confounding across stations, which single parameter survives as the driver? (isolation)
4. Does the direction of that effect match machining physics? (validity)
5. What in-line control, placed at the offending step, would have caught this earlier (the **escape point**), and would prevent recurrence?

| Persona | What they need from this analysis |
|---------|-----------------------------------|
| Quality Engineer / MRB | A defensible disposition and a closed root cause |
| Supplier Quality Engineer (customer side) | Evidence the corrective action is permanent, not cosmetic |

Writing the deliverable to satisfy *all four* is what makes it read as real quality-engineering work rather than a data exercise.

## 4. Business & regulatory significance

- **Cost of poor quality:** a mis-attributed root cause means the real driver keeps producing scrap and rework; every repeat 8D is wasted engineering time. Correct attribution stops the recurring loss.
- **Recurrence prevention:** the entire point of D7 ("prevent recurrence") is that the fix is systemic. A statistically proven D4 is what makes D7 credible to a customer.
- **Compliance:** AS9100 / ISO 9001 / IATF 16949 clause 10.2 requires corrective action traceable from the nonconformity to the eliminated cause. Auditors reject "we think it was operator error." A quantified driver with a control at the escape point is audit-grade evidence.
- **Customer expectation:** OEMs routinely mandate 8D (and, for aerospace engines, AS13100 mandates 8D-style problem solving) as the required format for supplier corrective actions.

## 5. Objective & guiding principle

Produce a **completed 8D Corrective Action Report** whose root cause (D4) is established by statistical evidence from a real dataset, and whose permanent corrective action (D5–D7) is a concrete, shop-floor control at the offending operation.

*Guiding principle:* every claim in the 8D is generated by the analysis notebook — no hand-typed numbers, no unsupported assertions.

## 6. Dataset rationale & contingency

**Preferred — CiP-DMD** (TU Darmstadt, *Center for industrial Productivity*): 847 pneumatic cylinders through a multi-step machining process, with **named** parameters and quality measurements linked by a traceability framework. The named parameters are the reason to prefer it — the root cause can be stated as a concrete, physical thing ("Operation 30, feed rate") instead of an anonymized index, which makes for a far stronger interview story. **Access caveat:** the public Zenodo record is the conference *paper*; the data files route through the InterQ project/authors, so **data availability is verified on Day 1 before committing.**

**Committed fallback — Bosch Production Line Performance** (Kaggle): ~1M real parts, hundreds of station-level measurements, a per-part pass/fail label. Guaranteed downloadable and structurally ideal for traceback; the only compromise is anonymized feature names (root cause is stated at the station/feature level). If CiP-DMD data can't be obtained quickly, the entire method runs unchanged on this set.

Either way, the *method* is identical; only the richness of the root-cause label changes. (Links and access notes in `resources.md`.)

## 7. Solution approach (overview — detail in `execution.md`)

1. **Simulate the MRB event** — filter to final-QC failures; treat as a quarantined nonconformance.
2. **Characterize the defect** — Pareto the failure modes; select the dominant one; establish baseline rate/volume.
3. **Screen upstream (univariate)** — test every upstream parameter for a pass-vs-fail difference, with multiple-comparison control.
4. **Isolate (multivariate)** — logistic regression with confounding + multicollinearity controls; cross-check with a tree-based model.
5. **Confirm** — effect size + significance + physics sanity check + escape-point identification.
6. **Author the 8D** — D0–D8, embedding the analysis as D4 evidence and an SPC control as D5–D7.

## 8. Assumptions & constraints

- The dataset's traceability keys correctly link each part's upstream parameters to its final result. (Verified in the data-dictionary step.)
- A single dominant root cause exists and is worth isolating; multi-cause interactions are a documented stretch, not the base case.
- This is a **retrospective case study on historical data**, not a live monitoring system — the corrective action is *recommended and justified*, not deployed.
- Time budget: 1–2 weeks part-time. Scope is deliberately bounded to one failure mode to fit.

## 9. Scope

**In scope:** one dominant failure mode; a defensible single-root-cause traceback; a full AIAG-format 8D; a reproducible analysis notebook; supporting figures.

**Out of scope (by design):** real-time/predictive monitoring; a deployed ML model; simultaneous multi-failure RCA; building a software tool. This is a *case study that ends in a result*, complementing (not duplicating) the `quality-platform` tool work.

## 10. Risk register

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|-----------|
| CiP-DMD data not publicly downloadable | Can't use preferred set | Day-1 access gate; committed Bosch PLP fallback (method unchanged) |
| Severe class imbalance (few failures) | Unstable coefficients / misleading tests | Class weighting; report effect sizes + CIs, not just p-values; consider balanced resampling for the cross-check model only |
| Confounding across correlated stations | Wrong parameter blamed | Multivariate model with station controls; VIF check; partial correlations |
| Multiple-comparison false positives | Spurious "significant" driver | Bonferroni / Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control on the univariate screen |
| Over-fitting the narrative | Story sounds better than the evidence | Physics sanity check; require agreement between logistic + tree-based models before concluding |
| Anonymized features (Bosch path) | Root cause less concrete | State cause at station/feature level; frame honestly as the dataset's limitation |

## 11. Deliverables & acceptance criteria

| Deliverable | Acceptance criteria |
|-------------|--------------------|
| **8D Report (PDF)** | All disciplines D1–D8 present; D4 states a specific station+parameter with effect size and p-value; D5–D7 give a concrete SPC control; readable by a non-analyst |
| **Analysis notebook** | Reruns top-to-bottom from raw data; every number in the 8D is produced here |
| **Figures** | Defect Pareto, parameter effect plot(s), and one "isolating" chart, all regenerated by the notebook |
| **README result summary + résumé bullet** | One-paragraph outcome + one quantified bullet |

## 12. Success metrics

- Root cause stated as a **specific station + parameter** with effect size and **p < 0.05** (after multiple-comparison control).
- Agreement between the logistic model and the tree-based cross-check on the top driver.
- Corrective action is concrete and shop-floor-implementable (named chart + step).
- End-to-end reproducibility from one notebook.
